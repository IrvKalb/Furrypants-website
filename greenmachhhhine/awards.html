Award Definitions

• Design Award: "The Design Award recipient demonstrates the ability to implement the most effective and efficient robot design process.  Their Engineering Notebook and discussion with the Judges will demonstrate the team's ability to produce a quality robot with minimal adult assistance."

• Educational Video: This is one of the VEX "Online Challenges" – a challenge posed by the VEX Robotics Competition that teams can complete with an online submission.  Teams were asked to "create a video to teach a new team something about the STEM aspects of robotics engineering or construction that you have learned from experience that can help them better prepare for a competition."  You can see our award-winning videos here: Bill Fly the Robot Guy: Triangles and Mythbusters: Wheels vs. Tank Treads.

• Essay Challenge: This is one of the VEX "Online Challenges" – a challenge posed by the VEX Robotics Competition that teams can complete with an online submission.  Teams were asked to "write a short essay on what exactly 'VEX Robotics' means to you."  You can read our award-winning essays here: A Solid Foundation and VEX Connects.

• Excellence Award: "This is the highest award presented in the VEX Robotics Competition. The recipient of this award is a team that exemplifies overall excellence in building a well-rounded VEX program. This team excels in many areas and is a shining example of dedication, devotion, hard work, and team work. A strong contender in numerous award categories, this team deserves to be recognized for their accomplishments building a robot, and in building a team committed to quality in everything they do."

• Highest Rookie Seed: At each FIRST tournament, teams are assigned a randomized schedule of alliance partners and opponents, and play a number of "qualification matches".  Teams are seeded (ranked) based on the restuls of those matches.  Once rankings are finalized, the highest-ranked first-year team receives the "Highest Rookie Seed" award.

• Programming Skills Challenge: In the Programming Skills Challenge, one robot is allowed on the field at a time.  The field is set up exactly as it would be during a regular match, and the team has exactly one minute for their robot to drive around the field autonomously (according to pre-programmed controls and without human intervention) and score as many points as possible.  Whichever team scores the most points in a single round of the Programming Skills Challenge is declared the Programming Skills Challenge winner for that competition.

• Robot Skills Challenge: In the Robot Skills Challenge, one robot is allowed on the field at a time.  The field is set up exactly as it would be during a regular match, and the team has exactly one minute to drive their robot around the field and score as many points as possible.  Whichever team scores the most points in a single round of the Robot Skills Challenge is declared the Robot Skills Challenge winner for that competition.

• Rookie All-Star Award: "Celebrates the rookie team exemplifying a young but strong partnership effort, as well as implementing the mission of FIRST to instpire students to learn more about science and technology."

• Think Award: "The 'Think' award is presented to a team that has successfully utilized autonomous programming modes during competition. Quality, consistency and success of autonomous programs in being an integral part of an effective strategy as well as the ability of the students to explain their programming process from strategy to syntax will help to determine a winner of this award. This award may be judged by the referees, programming inspectors and/or members of the judge panel."

• Tournament Champion: At each tournament, teams are assigned a randomized schedule of alliance partners and opponents, and play a number of "qualification matches".  Teams are seeded (ranked) based on the restuls of those matches.  Once rankings are finalized, the top 8 teams choose other teams to join their alliances.  Those alliances play out a single-elimination bracket against each other, and the teams on the alliance that emerges as the winner of that bracket are the touranment champions.

• Tournament Finalist: At each tournament, teams are assigned a randomized schedule of alliance partners and opponents, and play a number of "qualification matches".  Teams are seeded (ranked) based on the restuls of those matches.  Once rankings are finalized, the top 8 teams choose other teams to join their alliances.  Those alliances play out a single-elimination bracket against each other, and the teams on the alliance that make it to the finals but fall just short of winning the tournament are the touranment finalists.

• Website Challenge: This is one of the VEX "Online Challenges" – a challenge posed by the VEX Robotics Competition that teams can complete with an online submission.  Teams were asked to "develop a website that promotes your entire robotics program."  You can check out our award-winning website by looking around – you're already here!
